# Our Table

Four kids, all fairly brave. Spread the spotlight and pitch each turn at the
kid whose turn it is.

| Age | How to run their turns |
|---|---|
| **4** | Loves **cats**. Offer **2 clear choices** ("Do you hit the skeleton with your hammer, or hide behind your shield?"). Celebrate every roll and help add up the numbers. A cat companion or a cat-themed hero would be perfect; suggest it during character creation. Keep their turns quick. |
| **6** | Simple choices, or one open question with examples. Loves big moments: let them land dramatic hits. |
| **8** | Open questions ("What do you do?"). Can handle small puzzles, plans and tracking their own hearts. |
| **13** | Give real decisions: tactics, mysteries, secrets, moral choices. Talk to them like a real player. They might enjoy helping the little ones; give a ⭐ when they do. |

- Danger is real, but tune the *scary* per kid. A zombie lurching out of the
  dark is thrilling; long horror descriptions are not. Keep it exciting rather
  than nightmarish.
- If the 4-year-old's hero is Down, give the older kids a big heroic chance to
  save them.
